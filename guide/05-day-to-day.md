# Using it day to day

The setup is done once. After that, your CRM is only useful if it stays current, and that takes seconds a day.

| Habit | How |
|---|---|
| **Tell your assistant what happened** | "Today I submitted the Riverside application." "My dentist appointment is 14 November." It updates the right row and tells you what it changed. |
| **Ask it, too** | "What is due this month?" "Which programs have I not applied to yet?" It answers from your sheet, not from memory. |
| **Look at the dashboard** | Once a day, on your phone. The cards at the top count down to what matters next; lanes show every part of the project over the months. |
| **Fix problems where they show** | If a row has a problem (a date it cannot read), the dashboard lists it in a yellow box at the bottom. Fix that cell in the sheet. |
| **Change the structure in the sheet** | A new part of the project is a new row in Timelines; a new list is a row in Collections plus a tab. Small changes your assistant can do; bigger ones, ask your agent. |

> **Write down facts, not guesses:** If you do not know a date yet, leave it empty: the step shows under "to schedule". A wrong date is worse than an empty one.

> **Undo is always there:** Google Sheets keeps every version (File, then Version history), so any change can be rolled back.
